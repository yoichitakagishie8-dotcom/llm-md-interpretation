# Overall Behavior
The simulation traces a complete thermal cycle of a Ti powder bed: heating from ~331 K to a peak of ~2606 K (t≈84.8), followed by cooling back to a 600 K isothermal hold. Temperature rises linearly during heating, plateaus near 2600 K (indicating melting-associated energy absorption), then declines during cooling with a distinct plateau near 600 K (t≈160–240). P.E. rises monotonically from −225200 to a maximum of −205000 (heating) and recovers to −223000 on cooling. The order parameters q6 (bulk/surface) and Phase similarity confirm a crystalline→liquid→re-solidified sequence, with the final state structurally distinct from the initial (Phase COS ≈ +0.13–0.22, not 1.0).

# Event Identification
- **t≈20.8–32.8 (Powder densification/wetting):** Density Euclid dist jumps 34→217 and Density COS drops 0.97→0.15, signaling rapid initial morphological rearrangement (particle coalescence/necking) while q6 remains high (~0.42). This is a geometric, not crystallographic, change.
- **t≈50.8–68.8 (Melting/Order collapse):** Phase COS falls sharply 0.77→−0.32, crossing zero at t≈66.8; q6,Bulk drops 0.37→0.25 and q6,Surf 0.35→0.20. Surface melts ahead of bulk (surface premelting). MSD,Surf peaks (~1170 Å²) confirming high surface mobility.
- **t≈80.8–100.8 (Liquid plateau):** Temp saturates ~2600 K despite continued energy input; q6≈0.17 (liquid), MSD,Bulk maximal (~200–230 Å²). This plateau reflects latent heat of fusion absorption.
- **t≈140–162 (Recalescence/Solidification):** Temp arrests at ~600 K; q6,Bulk recovers 0.20→0.30; MSD collapses. Latent heat release during crystallization.
- **t≈162–240 (Isothermal crystal growth):** At constant ~600 K, q6,Bulk climbs 0.31→0.36 and Phase COS rises 0→0.22, indicating slow post-solidification ordering/grain refinement.

# Causal Relationship Reasoning
Temperature is the driving variable. **Cause→effect chains:** (1) ↑Temp → ↑K.E. (linear, 2036→16000) and ↑P.E. via anharmonic/bond-breaking. (2) Thermal energy → loss of long-range order: q6 and Phase COS decline as T→melting. (3) Surface atoms respond first (MSD,Surf ≫ MSD,Bulk throughout; surf/bulk ratio ~5–15), evidencing surface-initiated melting due to lower coordination. (4) The Temp plateau at 2600 K is *caused by* latent-heat buffering—energy input converts to configurational (P.E.) rather than kinetic. (5) On cooling, the 600 K arrest is recalescence: crystallization releases latent heat, decoupling Temp from monotonic cooling.

# Physical Conclusions
The data reproduce classical first-order melting/solidification thermodynamics: isothermal arrests coincide with P.E. inflections consistent with latent heat (ΔP.E. ≈ 20000 energy units across transition). Notably, the reported melting plateau (~2600 K) exceeds Ti's equilibrium T_m (1941 K), indicating **superheating** from finite heating rate and periodic constraints—a kinetic simulation artifact. Conversely, solidification at ~600 K reflects deep **undercooling** (~1340 K), typical of homogeneous nucleation barriers in fast-quenched MD. The decoupling of Density metrics (morphology, saturated early) from Phase metrics (crystallography, evolving later) demonstrates that geometric consolidation precedes crystallographic reorganization.
